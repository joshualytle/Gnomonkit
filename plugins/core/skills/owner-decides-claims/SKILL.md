# Facts the owner owns are asked for, never inferred

Wrong copy on a public site outlives the session that wrote it. It gets
indexed, quoted back by customers, and — for pricing, availability, or
compliance claims — can be a liability rather than an embarrassment.

## The split

This is the other half of `verify-before-asserting`. Together:

- **Checkable → check it.** If the repo, source docs, or a live system can
  answer it, answering from there is mandatory and asking the owner wastes
  their time.
- **Owner decision → ask.** If nothing can settle it, it is not a gap to fill
  with a plausible inference. Stop and ask.

## Always an owner decision

Pricing and discounts · launch status and dates · which platforms are
supported · which third-party integrations exist · security, encryption, and
compliance claims · legal, policy, and licensing text · competitor claims ·
years-of-experience and company-history figures.

**Never split the difference between two sources that disagree** — surface the
conflict instead. Two docs giving different numbers means one is stale, and
picking the average invents a third wrong answer.

## What this costs when skipped

A common failure: a company site describes a sibling product with a feature
set the product's own docs contradict — integrations it does not have, a data
source that appears nowhere upstream, an unqualified cross-platform claim for
an app that ships on two platforms on purpose, the wrong product category.
None of it is malicious; each line is a reasonable-sounding inference. All of
it ships and stays live until someone compares the two sources side by side.

## When NOT to use this

Anything a grep answers. Don't route checkable questions to the owner under
cover of this skill.
